## Observations (single-layer knockouts)
- Cross-frame edge, one layer at a time: the effect is concentrated in layers 7-17: L12 -6.6 % [-11.1, -2.9], L13 -6.3, L15 -6.3, L11 -3.7, L10 -3.1, L8 -2.5, L17 -4.0; layers 1-6 about 0 to -1 %; layers 18-28 ~0 (L18 -0.2, L19 -0.7, L20 -0.25).
- Video->text edge (all video keys), one layer at a time: almost everything within +-2 % except L15 -10.6 % [-18.5, -3.9]; other layers 6-20 are small and mostly include 0 => no single layer carries it (a band is needed: the band 6-20 gives -37 to -40 % in WP1/WP2).
- Temporal groups (video->text, layers 6-20, one of the 16 groups at a time): all within about -2 to +1.6 %; group 0 -0.18 % [-3.3, +2.9]; group 0 minus the mean of the other groups -0.06 pp [-3.5, +3.4]. A single group is not special; the group-0 effect seen in WP2 (about -9 to -14 pp when a window of many groups contains group 0) is therefore not a single-group effect but arises when a wide window includes group 0.
## Interpretation (not observation)
Cross-frame interactions matter in the middle layers (7-17), consistent with the paper's early/middle emphasis and with the Qwen half-blocking result; video->text dependence is distributed across layers 6-20 (no single layer, one exception L15). Blocking one temporal group of 16 barely changes the answer: the dependence is spread over time; the WP2 group-0 effect needs a wider window and may be an interaction (attention sink / position) rather than information in group 0. Limits: 60 videos, baseline-correct subset, one task, exploratory, per-arm CIs without multiplicity correction, 4-bit model. Not explanation faithfulness.
