# Paper-protocol analyses of Map the Flow on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and TVBench (written 2026-10-29 before the full run)

Purpose: reproduce, with the paper's protocol (read from the official repository, see experiments/2026-10-28_map_the_flow_reproduction_plan.md section 6) and the paper's benchmark, the analyses the paper reports in Figs 2, 3, 7, 8, 9, Tables 2 and B, the window ablation (Fig AB) and the failure cases (Sec 4), using the own implementation scripts/run_tvbench_paper_protocol.py on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. This is a different model from the paper's (LLaVA-NeXT-7B-Video-FT etc.): the question is whether the paper's *qualitative claims* hold in another VideoLLM on the same benchmark and prompt template, not whether its numbers are matched. Not done here: Table 3 pruning, logit lens on video tokens (Figs 4-5), attention maps (Fig 6), long-video / Video-MME / VCGBench / open-ended parts, last->last blocking (the knockout audit forbids the self cell).

## Fixed now
- Cohort: the same 400 TVBench videos as run_tvbench_pathway.py (4 tasks x 100, 8 frames; Action Antonym excluded). Qwen chat form of the paper's prompt (system sentence, `Question: ... Options: (A) ...`, `Only give the best option.`, assistant prefix `Best option:(`), option-letter readout, sdpa attention with explicit-mask hooks, fp32 readout, 4-bit weights.
- Knockouts: window k=9 centered at layers 2,5,...,26 (of 28), baseline-correct videos only for the full set (cross-frame, video->question, video->last, question->last, non-option question->last, true option->last, false options->last, video->non-option question, non-option question->true option, video->true option); cross-frame in the first half (layers 1-14) and second half (15-28) for Table 2 / B; cross-frame with k=1,5,13 at centers 5, 14, 23; layer-wise last-token logit lens for all videos; baseline-wrong videos: cross-frame, video->question, question->last (probability of the wrong prediction).
- Claims and the criteria that count as "holds" (fixed before seeing any full-run number; descriptive, no inferential test; paper values in brackets for reference only):
  1. Table 2: blocking cross-frame attention in the first half of the layers lowers accuracy among baseline-correct videos by at least 18% in every task [paper: 18.0-60.8%]; second half: at most 2% in every task [paper: 0.5-1.7%].
  2. Fig 2: cross-frame blocking effect peaks (largest drop) in layers below the middle (centers <= 14) and is within 5% of zero for centers >= 23.
  3. Fig 3: video->question reaches its maximal drop at a lower center than question->last; video->last stays within 5% of zero at every center.
  4. Fig 7: true option->last has a larger maximal drop than non-option question->last and than false option->last.
  5. Fig 8: direct video->true option vs the indirect route (video->non-option question, non-option question->true option) per task; the paper reports direct dominance in Action Antonym/Action Sequence/Scene Transition and indirect in Moving Direction/Object Count: reported per task, no pass/fail.
  6. Fig 9: the mean probability of the true option (baseline-correct videos) rises abruptly: the largest one-layer increase lies in layers 14-22 (of 28; the paper: around layer 20 of 32), and the mean true-option probability exceeds the mean false-option probability from that layer on.
  7. Fig AB: k=1 gives a markedly smaller mean drop than k=5, 9 and 13 at the centers where k=9 has its largest effect.
  8. Sec 4: in baseline-wrong videos the video->question and question->last patterns resemble those of baseline-correct videos; the cross-frame block raises the wrong-answer probability (case 2) in some and lowers it (case 1) in others; counts reported, no pass/fail.
- Each claim is reported as holds / does not hold / reported-only with the numbers; a claim failing is a result about Qwen2.5-VL, not a statement about the paper.

## Limits
One model of another family, 100 videos per task (the paper states no counts), 4 of 5 tasks, sdpa instead of eager (bf16-level numerics), centers every third layer, correct-only knockouts by the paper's design (so effects are conditional on a correct baseline), TVBench options order as given in the dataset, one seed-free deterministic run. The paper's own numbers cannot be matched with a different model; the strict reproduction needs the official checkpoint (blocked pending permission).

## Interpretation (inference; limited to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, TVBench 4 tasks, this prompt)
Most qualitative claims of the paper hold in a different VideoLLM on the paper's benchmark: early-to-middle cross-frame dependence, video-to-question integration in the middle layers, question-to-last in the mid-to-late layers, an unused direct video-to-last route, the true option carrying the question-to-last flow, bypass with narrow windows, and a failure-case pattern like the success pattern. Two do not: (i) second-half cross-frame blocking changes the predictions in three of four tasks (5-28%), far more than the paper's <= 1.7%; this is not a like-for-like test (our second half is layers 15-28 and still contains layers 15-20 where the centred windows show a cross-frame effect, the paper's second half is layers 17-32), a post hoc explanation not tested here; (ii) the layer-wise option probability rises only in the last layers instead of around layer 20 of 32 (~62% depth); the intermediate-layer logit lens of this model is not aligned with the vocabulary until late (probabilities ~0 for most layers), so this part says less about when the answer emerges than the paper's version does for its models. The task-dependence of direct vs indirect routes is partly reproduced. These are descriptive replications of qualitative claims, not matches of the paper's numbers.
