## Bounded novelty check

The primary-source check of [INFACT (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11481)
confirms it evaluates video QA under visual degradation, evidence corruption,
and temporal shuffling/reversal with Resist Rate and Temporal Sensitivity
Score. Its published description does not indicate a human-mapped explanation
claim-to-frame test or a temporally matched-control fixed-choice-margin
estimand. [EDCT (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00047) is closer at the
concept level: it extracts concepts from a target VLM's explanation and tests
them using edited images and judge-scored answer/explanation consistency. Its
reported setup is image OK-VQA, generated counterfactual edits, and LLM-assisted
scoring, not temporal frame masks or matched controls. This is a bounded
comparison of two papers, not a systematic literature review. It rules out a
broad novelty claim about explanation-driven VLM interventions; any narrower
novelty claim still needs a fuller search and direct method comparison.

A second targeted search found closer video-specific prior work. [VCSR
(ACM MM 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04224) explicitly selects causal
segments and frames for VideoQA and estimates intervention effects within a
trained architecture. [CRA (CVPR 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07635) is
closer still: it jointly predicts an answer and the time interval serving as
its rationale, and uses a front-door intervention with grounding and QA
outcomes on NExT-GQA/STAR. These methods make any broad novelty claim about
causal frame selection, temporal grounding, or video-QA evidence interventions
untenable. Their stated goal is to train/develop a VideoQG model, whereas the
current plan targets an external behavioral audit of an existing pretrained
VLM's own explanation claim using reviewer-mapped frames, matched temporal
controls, and a frozen-choice score. This distinction is a method comparison,
not yet a verified novelty claim.

[Causal-VidQA (CVPR 2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14895) adds an explicit
evidence-reasoning/explanation question category with reasons for some
commonsense tasks, but its abstract does not describe an intervention test of
the target VLM's rationale. [CausalPhys (KDD 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05966)
is a direct rationale-evaluation competitor: model rationales are judged
against expert causal graphs for entity faithfulness, relation awareness, and
description correctness over image/video physical-reasoning questions. The
published metrics assess semantic alignment to gold structure; they do not
appear to intervene on the same model input at the cited evidence location.
This project therefore cannot claim to be the first to assess causal rationale
faithfulness in video VLMs. The possible differentiator is measuring causal
dependence of the target model's own claim-mapped evidence with an external
frame intervention and a dose-matched control, rather than scoring semantic
rationale correctness alone. The search is targeted, not exhaustive, and the
causal interpretation of black/blur masks remains limited by OOD perturbation.

## Grounding metric contract

The official NExT-GQA evaluator's [evaluation code](https://github.com/doc-doc/NExT-GQA/blob/main/code/TempGQA/eval_ground.py)
loops over a question's gold spans, computes single-prediction temporal IoU
against each, and retains the maximum before averaging. It skips missing
prediction keys; that behavior is unsuitable for this project's frozen primary
denominator. The metric contract therefore uses the maximum IoU only over
reviewer-approved spans for the generated claim, while retaining missing or
invalid model intervals and claim mismatches at zero with distinct reason
codes. An unresolved mapping blocks scoring. Intervals must be finite and
positive-duration within video bounds; no clipping or endpoint reordering is
allowed. Point gold annotations block this contract. If the spans jointly
describe necessary moments rather than alternative acceptable locations, the
max-single-span metric is invalid and must be revised before outcomes.

Added a pure CPU helper and six synthetic tests for alternative-span maximum,
no overlap, parse/interval failures, claim mismatch/unverifiability,
fail-closed unresolved/malformed annotation handling, and finite-number
overflow. The independent audit found and helped repair an extreme finite
interval union-overflow case; the normalized calculation now passes its
regression. This does not inspect item-level annotations or verify that a
reviewer-approved span actually supports the claim. Focused tests pass (6), and
full scripts discovery passes (570); byte-compilation and diff checks pass.
The dated plan's gold-interval semantic audit remains a gate; this contract is
only a candidate arithmetic rule.

## Focused prior-art refresh

A bounded primary-source scan (2022–2026; VideoQA/VLM rationale or faithfulness
combined with temporal evidence, masking, ablation, or causal intervention)
found close overlap that narrows any defensible novelty claim. The strongest
new overlap is [CaST-Bench (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23216):
it supplies human-verified causal chains with time/box evidence, removes QA
items that remain answerable after masking all causal evidence, and evaluates
grounded chains jointly with answer correctness (including Faithful Answer
Rate). This is a dataset-construction completeness filter plus a grounded-chain
benchmark; it is not the proposed per-output matched-control score-difference
audit. [CREDiT (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09181) is a close
domain/method neighbor on NExT-GQA: it learns causal/non-causal frame-patch
representations and applies feature-level counterfactual interventions during
training/inference, reporting answer and grounded-QA performance. It does not
post-hoc test a reviewed natural-language claim from an existing generator.

Other relevant primary sources separate into adjacent methods: [INFACT
(2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11481) perturbs video evidence and temporal
order but evaluates answer reliability with Resist Rate / Temporal Sensitivity,
not claim-linked evidence attribution; [Journey Before Destination
(EACL 2026)](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.194.pdf) judges visual
faithfulness of step-level reasoning chains in image VQA, with localized
regeneration, but does not test video-frame deletion effects; [CRA (CVPR
2025)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Chen_Cross-modal_Causal_Relation_Alignment_for_Video_Question_Grounding_CVPR_2025_paper.html)
and [VCSR (2023)](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.04224) learn causal grounding or
scene/frame selection for VideoQA. These works rule out broad claims such as
“first causal video evidence intervention,” “first grounded video rationale
evaluation,” or “first video explanation faithfulness study.”

The only possible differentiator left by this bounded scan is a narrower
protocol combination: post-hoc, same-generator testing of whether frames
mapped to an independently reviewed natural-language answer claim reduce that
generator's fixed gold-vs-best-foil log margin more than an equal-count,
temporally matched control mask. This remains a candidate distinction, not a
firstness claim or a causal proof. It measures sensitivity to this particular
mask/control pair; masking can be out of distribution, control frames can
differ in content, and margin sensitivity does not establish the claim's truth,
completeness, or general explanation faithfulness. The search was targeted, not
systematic or exhaustive. No local data or model was accessed and no direct
faithfulness sample was run.

## Additional primary-source novelty challenge

The broader search added two close image-domain intervention studies that must
bound any description of the candidate contribution. The September 2026 paper [Counterfactual Tests for Measuring Chain-of-Thought
Faithfulness in Visual Language Models](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06704),
accepted to Findings of EMNLP 2026, adapts CT/CCT into
vCT/vCCT, uses edited image pairs on A-OKVQA and SNLI-VE, tests eight open
VLMs, measures prediction-distribution shift together with whether an edited
object is mentioned in generated explanations, and includes a same-edit-pipeline
reconstruction control. It reports that explanations can omit objects with
large score shifts and mention objects with small shifts; binary vCT can be
near-saturated. This directly rules out novelty claims about intervention-based
visual explanation faithfulness or same-generator explanation/visual-change
alignment in general. Its intervention is image-level object editing and its
claim-linked explanation/score association differs from the proposed fixed
post-hoc answer claim, temporal frame mask, and equal-count matched-control
margin contrast. It is not a video-frame study and its vCT/vCCT are
not equivalent to the proposed estimand.

[On the Faithfulness of Visual Thinking](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23482)
intervenes on visual and textual image-crop observations in multimodal
chain-of-thought, and separately judges visual evidence reliability and
sufficiency. It studies image-based zoom-in/tool-use reasoning (V*Bench and
HR-Bench), not ordinary video frames or natural-language answer-claim masks.
[Medical VLM reasoning faithfulness under multimodal perturbations](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11196)
uses paired chest-X-ray and prompt interventions, explanation scoring grounded
in a radiologist-authored knowledge base, and a four-radiologist reader study;
it does not use temporal video evidence or the same frame-mask margin contrast.
Together these sources confirm that explanation-specific visual interventions
and human-grounded multimodal faithfulness methods already exist. The only
remaining candidate distinction is the exact video-temporal protocol
combination stated above; it is not a priority/firstness claim. This scan is
still targeted rather than systematic, so an exhaustive novelty assertion is
unavailable.

## Primary-source novelty boundary and SESOI reassessment — 01:46 JST

“Caved or Convinced” (2026) studies user-supplied event-order claims under
frame reordering and fixed-budget sampling shifts. CRA (2025) jointly models
video question answers and temporal intervals with a learned causal alignment
intervention. STEAR (2026) uses token-conditioned spatiotemporal evidence and
localized temporal counterfactual decoding for hallucination mitigation.
EDCT-Bench (2026) is close at the concept level: it extracts cited visual
concepts from explanations, makes verified minimal image edits, and checks
answer/explanation consistency; its reported domains are image tasks, not
temporal video intervals.
VideoSEAL (ICML 2026) measures whether answers are supported by inspected
long-video evidence; temporal grounding consistency work tests models against
their own predicted moments; ViTED (CVPR 2025) produces temporal evidence
chains and evaluates their quality. FIFA in particular means temporal claim
verification against video is not itself a new contribution.

Across the inspected methods, the only defensible distinction still visible
is a narrow operational combination: freeze one generated answer claim per
video; independently review its claim-specific evidence interval; mask the

versus best-foil log-probability margin. This is a bounded search result, not
a proof of global novelty or priority. Do not claim “first,” temporal
faithfulness novelty, or general explanation-faithfulness novelty. The
proposal for hidden-state propagation is a separate intervention question
