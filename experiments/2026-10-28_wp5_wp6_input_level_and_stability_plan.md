## WP5: input-level intervention instead of an attention-mask edit
Why: WP2/WP3 edit the attention mask (out-of-distribution, criticized in the faithfulness-metric literature and by reviewers of the base paper). WP5 asks whether the same dependence on the model's own cited interval appears when the **input frames** are changed with the frame count, token layout and positions unchanged.
- Cohorts: WP2 test (278 requested) and WP2 val (300 requested), as in WP2/WP3. The cited groups (one group = 2 frames) are read from the WP2 receipts by media hash (greedy decoding is deterministic: the WP2 and WP3 citations were identical in 265/265 and 289/289 videos), so WP5 runs no generation. Videos with an unusable citation in WP2 are excluded identically (same analysed sets).
- Frame operation `replace(S)`: every frame of the groups in S is replaced by a copy of the nearest frame outside S (ties -> the earlier frame); timestamps and the 32-frame geometry are unchanged.
- Arms per video (8 forward passes, fp32 readout, sdpa, no attention-mask edit): baseline; N_cited = replace(cited groups); N_sham = replace(equal-count disjoint hash sham of WP2); S_cited = keep-only-cited = replace(all groups except cited); S_sham = keep-only-sham; reference arms: N_ann = replace(annotation groups), S_ann = keep-only-annotation; positive control for necessity: allstatic = every frame replaced by the middle frame.
- Primary 1 (necessity, comparable to WP2): effect = -( (self-margin change under N_cited) - (self-margin change under N_sham) ), self-margin as in WP2. Primary 2 (sufficiency, comparable to WP3): gold margin under S_cited minus under S_sham. Decision rule per primary, identical to WP2/WP3: supported iff t, sign-flip and Wilcoxon p < 0.05 and mean >= 0.3 nat and the positive control holds (necessity: allstatic mean relative change of the predicted option probability <= -20% on baseline-correct videos; sufficiency: S_ann minus S_sham mean >= 0.3 nat); else TOST at +-0.3 nat or inconclusive. Holm over the two primaries within a cohort.
- Secondary (descriptive): per-video Spearman between the attention-level effect (WP2 necessity / WP3 sufficiency) and the input-level effect on the same videos (cross-method agreement), accuracy flips with exact McNemar, split by baseline correctness and by overlap of the citation with the annotation.
- Each cohort is analysed once, not pooled. Workload about 8 forwards + 8 input preparations per video, estimated 25-30 s/video, ~2 h per cohort; smoke n=3 first.
- Limits: frame replacement by a neighbour frame is an in-distribution edit of the input but changes motion cues and may remove information the model never used; the frame-level granularity is 2 frames (one temporal group); sham differs in position and content; one model, one citation format; forced-choice endpoint; label-free; measures dependence on the cited interval, not a faithfulness verdict.

## WP6: stability of the citation across option order
Why: a citation that changes when only the order of the answer options changes cannot faithfully reflect the evidence the model used. Uses the same cyclic option-order shifts as PERM (shift s in 1..4: options[(i+s) mod 5]), the WP2 generation prompt and parser, greedy decoding, no intervention.
- Cohorts and exclusions as WP5 (same analysed sets); the baseline citation (shift 0) is read from the WP2 receipt; 4 new generations per video (~7 s each, ~30 s/video, ~2.2 h per cohort).
- Quantities per video and shift: (a) group-level IoU between the cited groups at shift s and at shift 0; (b) whether the answer letter, mapped back to the original option, equals the shift-0 letter; (c) whether the citation parses and is usable.
- Primary (descriptive with a null, one analysis per cohort): mean IoU over shifts and videos, compared with a permutation null that pairs each video's shift-0 citation with the shift-s citation of a different random video (1000 permutations; the null keeps the marginal citation lengths and locations); stable iff the observed mean IoU exceeds the null mean by >= 0.2 (absolute) with a bootstrap 95% interval over videos above 0.1. The practical threshold 0.2 is fixed now as the smallest difference at which stability is visible relative to the typical group-level IoU of the citations with the annotation (0.29 in WP2/WP3). This is a reliability measurement; a citation can be stable and unfaithful.
- Secondary (descriptive): mean pairwise IoU between all five orders; letter-consistency rate; stability split by baseline correctness and by overlap of the shift-0 citation with the annotation; whether the WP2 necessity effect is larger for stable citations (Spearman of per-video stability with the necessity effect).
- Limits: greedy decoding yields one sample per order; unusable generations reduce the analysed set per shift (reported); one model and format; label-free.

## Result: WP5 on the test cohort (278 recorded, 13 excluded, 265 analysed; 2026-10-28 05:58 UTC; one prespecified analysis; the baseline forward reproduced the WP2 baseline exactly, max |dlogp| = 0.0; results/wp5test_summary_20261028q_wp5test.json)
- **Necessity (replace cited vs replace sham, self-margin nat):** +0.356 [+0.172, +0.541], t p=1.8e-4, sign-flip p=1e-4, Wilcoxon p=0.0023, Holm p=3.6e-4. The prespecified positive control **failed narrowly** (allstatic: mean relative change of the predicted option probability -18.7% on baseline-correct videos vs the required <= -20%), so by the rule fixed before the run the necessity result is **uninformative / not claimable as supported**; the numbers are reported descriptively only. (Replacing all frames with the middle frame leaves a static view of the scene, which is probably less destructive than the whole-video attention knockout of WP2; the -20% gate was carried over from that attention-level control without a calibration for this input operation.)
- **Sufficiency (keep cited vs keep sham, gold margin):** +0.218 [-0.078, +0.514], t p=0.149, TOST p=0.29 -> **inconclusive** (positive control passed: keep-annotation minus keep-sham +0.573 nat).
- Descriptive: necessity effect with citation-annotation overlap +0.451 (n=150) vs without +0.234 (n=115). Accuracy vs baseline 203/265: replace_cited 192 (+7/-18, McNemar p=0.043), replace_sham 197 (+0/-6, p=0.031), keep_cited 192 (+9/-20, p=0.061), keep_sham 198 (+13/-18, p=0.47), replace_annotation 193 (+3/-13, p=0.021), keep_annotation 205 (+17/-15, p=0.86), allstatic 172 (+12/-43, p=3e-5).
- **Cross-method agreement per video (secondary):** Spearman between the attention-level effect (WP2/WP3) and the input-level effect on the same 265 videos: necessity +0.487 [+0.370, +0.596], sufficiency +0.447 [+0.332, +0.563].
- Interpretation (inference): the input-level intervention gives an effect of the same sign and similar size as the attention-level one (about +0.36 vs +0.31 nat for necessity), and the per-video agreement between the two methods is moderate (0.45-0.49), so the dependence on the cited interval is not just an artefact of the attention-mask edit; but the formal necessity claim is blocked by the failed positive-control gate, and the sufficiency contrast does not reach the threshold at the input level.
